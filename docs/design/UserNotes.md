## 2. Expanded plan (sections 6-12 as shared)

```text
6. Equipment, loot and enchantments
Here I would slightly modify your original enchantment idea.

The Hunger Games universe does not really use magical weapon enchantments. Instead, create a Capitol equipment modification and special equipment system.

Categories:
Category	Examples
Weapons	Bows, knives, spears, axes.
Protection	Armour, protective clothing.
Survival	Backpacks, food, water.
Medical	Bandages, medicines, treatment supplies.
Traps	Snares, tripwires and improvised traps.
Special equipment	Blow darts, technical equipment and rare tools.
Cosmetic modifications	Weapon appearances, visual trails and effects.

Your enchantment drops can become rare equipment modifications.

For instance:
- Improved weapon durability.
- Enhanced camouflage.
- Special arrow types.
- Improved trap effectiveness.
- Increased carrying capacity.
- Improved medical equipment.
These should feel like equipment from Panem, not fantasy magic.
7. Economy, Gold and Robux
Your original monetisation ideas remain useful, but they should be organised around the Capitol's economy and the player's progression.
7.1 Two currencies
Currency	Purpose
Gold	Earned through gameplay and used for ordinary purchases.
Robux	Used for premium purchases and monetisation.

7.2 Gold acquisition
- Winning Games.
- Completing missions.
- Daily objectives.
- Arena achievements.
- Discovering hidden supplies.
- Completing campaign chapters.

8. Game modes

The premium mode should ideally offer additional experiences rather than exclusive competitive advantages.
9. Long-term progression and unlockables
Players should have reasons to continue playing after winning their first Games.

I would introduce:
- Account levels.

- Character unlocks.
- Character-specific progression.
- Equipment collections.
- Achievement systems.
- Campaign completion.
- Historical Games completion.
- Cosmetic collections.
- Seasonal challenges.
For example, winning the Second Quarter Quell campaign could unlock Haymitch as a playable character in compatible modes.

11. One important distinction: Canon versus original additions
I would maintain three classifications throughout development.
Classification	Meaning	Example
Canon	Directly established in the books and films.	Reaping, sponsors, mutts, Cornucopia.
Canon-inspired	New mechanics consistent with the universe.	District-based skill specialisations.
Original / alternative	New content created for the game.	Killing Snow, custom enchantments, alternative endings.

This will help ensure that the game remains recognisably Hunger Games while still allowing you to introduce original mechanics.

One further consideration: If this is intended for public commercial release on Roblox, using the Hunger Games name, characters, settings and distinctive protected elements requires appropriate intellectual-property permission. A private prototype and an officially licensed commercial game are different matters.

12. My recommended overall vision
I would make the Second Quarter Quell the foundational game experience, because it gives you a defined arena, a historically significant competition, a large number of tributes and particularly interesting environmental mechanics.

Then build outward:

LONG-TERM EXPANSION

New historical Games · Districts · Characters · Arenas · Storylines


The most important thing is that the arena should not simply be a map in which players fight. It should feel like a controlled spectacle: the Capitol watches, the Gamemakers intervene, sponsors influence survival, alliances form and the environment is deliberately manipulated.

That is what will make the game feel genuinely connected to the world of The Hunger Games.
```
