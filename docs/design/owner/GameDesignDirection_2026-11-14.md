THE HUNGER GAMES — GAME DESIGN DIRECTION
GAMEPLAY SYSTEMS · WORLD DESIGN · PROGRESSION · ECONOMY · VISUAL DIRECTION






















10














1. Overall philosophy and visual direction
The game should be approached as an attempt to recreate the atmosphere and beauty of the Hunger Games universe, rather than simply translating its mechanics into Roblox.

Roblox Studio is the medium through which the game is expressed, not the limit of what the game should aspire to look like.

The objective should be to produce something visually compelling, semi-realistic and recognisably inspired by the cinematography of the films. Environments should feel like actual places rather than conventional game maps. Forests should feel dense, animals should feel alive, and the arena should feel like a carefully constructed ecosystem.

I would establish three priorities:
1. Visual quality: Every environment, animal, object and structure should be designed with considerable attention to detail.
2. Natural gameplay: Interactions should make sense within the world. Players should be able to climb trees, hunt animals, collect resources, find shelter and navigate the environment organically.
3. Simplicity of systems: The underlying mechanics should be relatively straightforward. The complexity should come from how players interact with the world, not from overwhelming them with menus, currencies, abilities and unnecessary statistics.
1.1 Asset creation and 3D modelling
The game's most important visual assets should be reconstructed and developed in Blender before being imported into Roblox Studio.

This includes:
- Mutated animals and arena creatures.
- Ordinary wildlife, including squirrels, birds and other forest animals.
- Trees, vegetation, rocks and terrain details.
- Weapons, backpacks and survival equipment.
- Cornucopia structures.
- Arena installations and environmental hazards.
- Special environmental event assets.
Animals, in particular, should not look tacky, cartoonish or like generic Roblox NPCs. They should have believable proportions, anatomy, textures and movement.

The intention is to create semi-realistic models with carefully considered materials, lighting and animations. A squirrel should look and behave like an actual squirrel; a mutated animal should look like a deliberately engineered creature that belongs in the Hunger Games universe.

The same standard should apply to the environment. Trees should have varied shapes and foliage, water should have depth and movement, and the forest floor should contain smaller details that make the world feel inhabited.






















12











The visual quality should be consistent throughout the game. It would be counterproductive to have a beautifully constructed forest populated by poorly modelled animals or surrounded by simplistic environmental assets.

2. Arena gameplay and environmental interaction






















10











The arena should feel like a functioning natural environment that happens to be controlled by the Capitol.

Players should not simply run around a map looking for other players to eliminate. They should have the freedom to explore, establish temporary shelter, gather resources, hunt, avoid danger and decide how they want to survive.
2.1 Cornucopia and starting equipment
The Cornucopia should be the focal point of the beginning of each match.

Players begin on individual launch platforms, positioned around the central structure. Inside and around the Cornucopia are weapons, backpacks, food, water and other survival equipment.

When the countdown ends, players must decide whether to approach the Cornucopia or escape into the surrounding environment.

There should be meaningful differences between the available equipment. Some items should be immediately useful, while others should require players to take greater risks to obtain them.

The opening should feel tense and unpredictable, rather than like an ordinary countdown followed by a conventional team fight.
2.2 Natural environmental interaction
This is an area I would particularly emphasise.

Players should be able to interact with the environment beyond simply walking and jumping through it.

For example:
- Climbing trees: Players can climb suitable trees to escape predators, observe surrounding areas or conceal themselves.
- Hunting: Players can hunt squirrels, birds and other small animals for food.
- Gathering: Players can collect suitable natural resources.
- Exploration: Players can discover hidden locations, caves, water sources and abandoned structures.
- Shelter: Players can use natural cover and suitable environmental structures to conceal themselves.
- Tracking: Players can observe animal movements and potentially identify the presence of other players.
- Water: Players can find rivers, lakes and other water sources.
These interactions should be implemented naturally. For example, climbing should use the geometry and structure of the tree, rather than an obvious floating ladder or artificial climbing interface.

Hunting should also require some degree of skill. Players might use bows, traps or other equipment to catch animals. Wildlife should have believable behaviour, such as fleeing when approached or hiding when threatened.






















9











The intention is to make the arena feel like somewhere a person could genuinely attempt to survive.
2.3 Gamemaker-controlled challenges
The Gamemakers should be able to manipulate the arena through environmental interventions.

This is one of the defining features of the Hunger Games and should be reflected in the gameplay.

Rather than introducing constant random disasters, events should occur periodically and have a reason for existing. They should create tension, force players to reconsider their positions and prevent matches from becoming stagnant.

Potential events include:
Event	Gameplay effect
Mutts released	Engineered creatures enter designated areas and pursue tributes.
Toxic pollen	Certain areas become dangerous to enter.
Poisonous water	Previously usable water sources become hazardous.
Artificial fire	Fires spread through sections of the forest.
Flooding	Low-lying areas become inaccessible.
Dangerous wildlife	Mutated animals begin appearing in particular locations.
Arena manipulation	Certain passages or locations become inaccessible.
Electrical interference	Certain arena systems or areas become hazardous.

Events should be visually impressive and properly constructed.

For example, a mutated creature should not simply appear as a generic NPC with an enlarged health bar. It should have its own model, animations, movement patterns, sound design and behavioural characteristics.
2.4 Event frequency and the arena boundary
The arena should gradually become more dangerous, but the pace should be deliberately controlled.

The playable area should not shrink too quickly.

Players need enough time to explore, hunt, collect resources, form alliances and establish themselves within the environment.

A rapidly shrinking boundary would undermine the slower survival-oriented gameplay that makes the arena interesting.

Instead:
- The initial arena should provide considerable freedom.
- Environmental events should gradually encourage movement.
- The Gamemakers can introduce hazards in selected locations.
- The playable area can become more restricted as the match progresses.
- The final stages should naturally bring surviving tributes closer together.
The boundary should feel like an actual technological containment system, rather than an arbitrary battle-royale circle.

3. Character development and skill progression






















7











I would keep character development relatively simple.

There is no need to introduce a complicated character-class system or give every character special supernatural abilities. The Hunger Games is fundamentally about human survival, resourcefulness, physical ability and strategy.

Instead, players should develop their physical attributes through a straightforward training system.
3.1 Physical attributes
I would introduce four principal attributes:
Attribute	What it affects
Stamina	How long a player can run, climb and perform physically demanding actions.
Strength	Physical interactions, carrying capacity and certain combat interactions.
Hunger	The player's nutritional needs and ability to sustain themselves.
Thirst	The player's hydration needs and ability to remain active.

Hunger and thirst are survival needs rather than conventional combat statistics.

Players must regularly find food and water to maintain them. Stamina and strength represent their physical development.
3.2 Training and progression
Introduce a separate training environment where players can improve their physical attributes.

Possible training activities:
- Running and endurance exercises.
- Weight training.
- Climbing exercises.
- Archery practice.
- Weapon handling.
- Physical obstacle courses.
Completing training activities awards players progression tokens or training coins.

These can then be spent on improving their attributes.

For example:
- Increasing stamina capacity.
- Improving stamina recovery.
- Improving strength.
- Increasing the efficiency of physical actions.
- Improving hunger or thirst management.
The progression should be noticeable without becoming excessive.

A player who has trained should have a genuine advantage in certain situations, but should not become so powerful that a new player has no chance of surviving against them.
3.3 Character progression purchases
Players should also have the option to purchase additional training tokens using Robux.

However, the system must be carefully balanced.

Purchasing tokens should accelerate progression, not remove the need to play the game.

Attribute improvements should have defined maximums, and the differences between lower-level and higher-level players should remain relatively modest.

The objective is to provide a convenient progression option, not to create an environment where financial spending determines who wins the Hunger Games.

4. Loot and equipment






















6











Equipment should feel like physical objects that belong in the world.

I would avoid introducing an enormous inventory of artificial items or hundreds of unnecessary equipment variations.

The focus should be on a smaller collection of useful objects, each with a clear purpose.
4.1 Equipment categories
Weapons
- Bows and arrows.
- Knives.
- Axes.
- Spears.
- Other appropriate weapons.
Survival equipment
- Backpacks.
- Food.
- Water containers.
- Basic tools.
- Shelter-related materials.
Medical supplies
- Bandages.
- Medicines.
- Medical kits.
- Other recovery items.
Special equipment
- Traps.
- Special ammunition.
- Rare tools.
- Equipment designed for particular environmental challenges.
4.2 Equipment drops and rarity
Equipment can be found in the Cornucopia, hidden locations, supply drops and other parts of the arena.

Different items can have different rarity levels.

However, rarity should primarily represent availability, quality and usefulness, rather than introducing fantasy-style magical properties.

For example, a particularly rare bow might have superior construction or durability, rather than inexplicably dealing magical damage.
4.3 Rare modifications
Instead of conventional enchantments, introduce limited equipment modifications.

These could include:
- Improved weapon durability.
- Different arrow types.
- Better camouflage materials.
- Improved carrying equipment.
- More effective traps.
These modifications should be grounded in the technological and material world of Panem.

5. Economy and monetisation
The monetisation should feel like a natural extension of the game rather than something that constantly interrupts the experience.

I would avoid excessive pop-ups, exaggerated offers, artificial scarcity and gimmicky premium systems.

The player should primarily experience the game as a survival competition. Purchases should be optional and unobtrusive.
5.1 In-game currency — Gold
Introduce a simple in-game currency, which players can accumulate through ordinary gameplay.

Gold could be earned through:
- Completing matches.
- Winning or placing highly.
- Completing training activities.
- Achieving particular objectives.
- Completing challenges.
- Progressing through certain game modes.
Gold can then be spent on selected items and progression features.

It should be a relatively straightforward economy rather than an elaborate trading system with multiple currencies and complicated exchange rates.
5.2 Purchasing Gold with Robux
Players should be able to purchase Gold through Roblox's developer products.

Introduce several purchase sizes:
Package	Purpose
Small	Affordable entry-level purchase.
Medium	Larger currency purchase.
Large	Bulk purchase.
Special	Occasionally available themed bundle.

The packages should be clearly priced and easy to understand.

There is no need to introduce numerous currencies or complicated conversion systems.
5.3 Training tokens
Training tokens should be another potential purchase category.

Players can earn them through training and use them to improve their physical attributes.

Alternatively, players can purchase additional tokens with Robux to accelerate their progression.

This should be implemented with sensible caps, diminishing returns where appropriate and a modest difference between spending and non-spending players.

The purpose is to offer convenience, not an overwhelming competitive advantage.
5.4 Game passes and premium access
Game passes should provide lasting, understandable benefits.

Potential examples:
- Access to an additional game mode.
- Additional character customisation options.
- Exclusive private arena settings.
- Additional cosmetic collections.
- Special training environments.
Premium access should not automatically make someone stronger or give them an unfair advantage in ordinary Hunger Games matches.

6. Cosmetics and visual customisation


































Cosmetics are where I would concentrate a substantial portion of the game's optional monetisation.

The important thing is to maintain the visual identity of the universe.

I would not introduce excessive colourful effects, exaggerated glowing weapons or conventional fantasy-game cosmetics that undermine the atmosphere.
6.1 Weapon visual effects
Players should be able to purchase cosmetic effects for their weapons using Robux.

For example:
- Subtle weapon trails.
- Unique arrow-release effects.
- Special impact effects.
- Different weapon finishes.
- Distinctive weapon animations.
- Subtle particle effects.
These should be carefully designed and visually sophisticated.
6.2 Trails
I agree that ordinary in-game trails are largely unnecessary.

The game should not constantly display colourful lines behind players or weapons.

If trails are introduced as part of the natural environment or weapon animations, they should be restrained.

For example:
- A subtle wind-like trail.
- A brief motion distortion.
- A faint dust effect.
- A natural particle displacement.
- A subtle visual indication of an arrow moving through the air.
The objective is to enhance the visual presentation, not make every movement look like a fantasy ability.
6.3 Cosmetic monetisation
I would make weapon effects, specialised trails and certain cosmetic finishes purchasable using Robux.

The player should be buying an aesthetic variation, not a stronger weapon.

Cosmetics should be designed as actual additions to the visual quality of the game, rather than cheap recolours created solely to generate purchases.

7. How all the systems work together
The experience should feel cohesive rather than like a collection of disconnected Roblox mechanics.

Consider what happens when a player enters a match.
Example player experience
1. Entering the arena
   
   The player appears on their launch platform. The Cornucopia is visible in the distance, containing weapons, backpacks and supplies.
2. Initial survival
   
   The player decides to avoid the initial confrontation and escapes into the forest, searching for water and shelter.
3. Exploration
   
   They climb a tree, observe the surrounding area and spot another tribute moving through the forest.
4. Hunting
   
   The player discovers squirrels and uses their available equipment to hunt one, obtaining food.
5. Gamemaker intervention
   
   A warning is broadcast. A group of engineered creatures is being released into the area. The player must relocate.
6. Survival strategy
   
   The player navigates through the forest, avoids the creatures and finds a new location with access to water.
7. Competition
   
   Other tributes begin approaching. Alliances, confrontations and strategic decisions determine who survives.
8. Progression
   
   After the match, the player receives Gold and experience. They can use their rewards to develop their physical attributes or purchase cosmetic items.

This is the kind of gameplay loop I would aim for: exploration, survival, tension, environmental interaction and competition, with progression outside the match.

8. Final design principles
I would establish these as non-negotiable guidelines for development.
Principle	Development direction
Visual excellence	Prioritise high-quality Blender assets, textures, lighting and animations.
Semi-realism	Avoid cartoonish animals, exaggerated effects and generic Roblox aesthetics.
Natural interaction	Make climbing, hunting, gathering and exploration meaningful.
Simple progression	Keep attributes and currencies understandable.
Balanced monetisation	Purchases should be optional and not excessively powerful.
Authenticity	Keep the mechanics and equipment consistent with Panem.
Atmosphere	Use environmental sound, lighting, weather and cinematography to create tension.
Replayability	Vary arena events, locations, equipment and player interactions.

9. What I would prioritise building first
I would not begin by developing every progression and monetisation system. I would first establish a visually convincing, playable arena.
Development priorities
Recommended order

1. Arena and environment
   
   Construct the forest, terrain, water, vegetation, Cornucopia and environmental structures. Establish the visual style.
2. Player interaction
   
   Implement movement, climbing, hunting, resource collection, inventory and basic survival.
3. Animals and environmental events
   
   Create realistic wildlife, mutated creatures, animations and Gamemaker-controlled interventions using Blender assets.
4. Combat and match structure
   
   Implement weapons, damage, elimination, starting platforms and the overall match lifecycle.
5. Progression
   
   Add Gold, training tokens, stamina, hunger, thirst and strength development.
6. Monetisation and cosmetics
   
   Introduce Robux purchases, weapon cosmetics, trails, game passes and optional premium experiences.

The fundamental idea: Build a beautiful and believable Hunger Games arena first. Make the world interesting enough that players want to explore it even when they are not fighting anyone. Then introduce the survival mechanics, competition, progression and monetisation around that world.
