Written by `tools/map1/pack.py`. Do not edit: run the packer again. Heights and levels are in studs; a cell is one
4-stud voxel column, `x = -2752 + 4 * (col + 0.5)`, `z = -2752 + 4 * (row + 0.5)`, row 0 in the north.

Read from: `Map1_Height.png` (sha256 bc9b751905d76447...) and `Map1_WaterLevel.png` (sha256 6b6ea1795bb88dad...).

## The rules (Map1Build.md, decision 5)

1. Every water level sits 0.5 to 2 studs into its 4-stud voxel layer.
2. Two neighbouring wet cells have the same level, or a dry sill cell stands between them.
3. No dry cell next to water is lower than that water's level.

"Neighbouring" means the 8 cells round a cell, diagonals included: the stricter reading. A workbench test on
15 November 2026 found no water face at a corner even when only the four side neighbours were sealed, so the
diagonals are a margin, not a need.

## Repairs: 327 cells

281 cells beside narrow water were made wet (the margin: see below), 281 of them lowered, by at most 2.94 studs.
20 cells were dried and 46 heights were raised (the largest rise is 2.16 studs). Nothing else was lowered, no
other water was added, and no material or region id was changed: a sill keeps the slot its cell had.

### The margin: 281 cells

Terrain water in a channel 2 or 3 cells wide is partly hidden by its own banks: the bank's surface leans over
the first wet cell on each side. Measured in Studio with the game's voxel rules, a channel 2 cells wide on a
diagonal shows no water at all when its level sits 1 stud or less into its voxel layer, and one 3 cells wide
shows about half. With one more wet cell on each side, 86 to 100% of the designed water shows. So every dry
cell at the side of a narrow pool (fewer than 2,000 cells: the stream's pools, not the lake) becomes wet at that
pool's level, 0.25 studs deep: too shallow to be taken for a drinking spot, deep enough to be a wet column.
A bank more than 2.75 studs above the water is left alone (no cut deeper than 3 studs), and a margin cell that
would touch lower water stays dry and becomes part of the sill instead.
